This topic is about the trade-off after supply shocks. A cost-push shock moves inflation and output in opposite directions, inflation up and output down when costs rise. No policy rate brings both back to target at once. A demand shock moves them in the same direction. The mandate weighs inflation against output, and the debrief at the end of the term shows what each choice cost.
Typical mistakes, each with a hint:
1. Raising the policy rate until inflation is back at the target, whatever this does to output. Hint: output against its trend and unemployment.
2. Ignoring inflation to protect output. Hint: inflation against the target.
3. Treating the fall in costs like a rise in demand. Hint: check whether inflation and output move in the same or in opposite directions.
