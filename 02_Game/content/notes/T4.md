This topic is about forward guidance at the floor. At the floor the policy rate cannot be lowered further, and the public expects it to stay at zero for a known number of quarters. A credible promise to keep it at zero for longer lowers the real interest rates the public expects for the coming quarters, which supports spending, output and inflation today.
Typical mistakes, each with a hint:
1. Promising only what the public already expects. Hint: compare the length of the promise with the number of quarters the public already expects.
2. Raising the policy rate early once the economy recovers. Hint: the record judges every announced quarter.
3. Promising zero for very long and letting inflation overshoot. Hint: expected inflation.
