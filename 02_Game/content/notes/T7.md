This topic is about electoral pressure. In the last quarters before an election, the party in office may increase government spending. If the central bank lets the extra spending lower unemployment, support for the party rises. A central bank that moves its rate with the rate the economy needs offsets the effect. After a re-election, voters treat the next pre-election spending with caution.
Typical mistakes, each with a hint:
1. Keeping the policy rate unchanged while the extra spending runs. Hint: spending that people expect to end after the election raises the rate the economy needs.
2. Overreacting after the election. Hint: the extra spending ends with the election.
