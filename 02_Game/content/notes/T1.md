This topic is about monetary policy with lags. A demand shock changes how much households and firms want to spend, and with it the rate the economy needs. A policy rate that moves with the rate the economy needs keeps output at its potential and inflation at the target. A decision shows in next quarter's figures, and wages keep adjusting for several quarters after.
Typical mistakes, each with a hint:
1. Reacting to last quarter's figures although the shock has already faded. Hint: demand shocks fade within a few quarters, and the briefing says whether spending has changed again.
2. Changing the policy rate too little after a large shock. Hint: compare how far output moved away from its trend with the size of the shock the briefing reports.
3. Expecting an effect in the same quarter. Hint: this quarter's figures show the effect of last quarter's decision.
