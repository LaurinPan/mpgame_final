This topic is about fiscal policy. A temporary increase in government spending raises demand and output, and it takes away some of what households would have consumed. While people expect the extra spending to shrink back, the rate the economy needs is higher, so a policy rate that stays unchanged lets output rise above its potential.
Typical mistakes, each with a hint:
1. Ignoring the government's extra spending. Hint: the newspaper's box on government spending and its politics section.
2. Treating the extra spending as permanent. Hint: it shrinks back to normal over the coming years, and so does its effect on the rate the economy needs.
