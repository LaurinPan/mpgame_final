This topic is about expectations. The public reads the central bank's aim from every policy rate it sets. A rate higher than the public expected suggests a lower aim, and the public moves its view a small step towards that signal each quarter. Bringing the public's view back to the target can cost output while it moves.
Typical mistakes, each with a hint:
1. Lowering the policy rate as soon as inflation falls, while the public still believes in a high aim. Hint: the dashed line of the public's view of our aim on the chart of expected inflation.
2. Tightening hard and then easing again. Hint: the public changes its view slowly, so a steady course convinces it more.
3. Waiting for words to help. Hint: there are no announcements in this topic.
