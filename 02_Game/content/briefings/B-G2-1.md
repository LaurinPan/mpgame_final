Firms' costs have risen across the economy, for example because energy has become more expensive, and firms pass higher costs on to their prices, so inflation rises while output falls. No policy rate can stop both at once: a higher rate lowers inflation but deepens the fall in output, and a lower rate does the opposite. We set the policy rate to {guided.rate} percent, {guided.change}, since a higher rate lowers inflation only slowly and costs output at once, so we move it a little and let the shock fade. The debrief at the end of the term will show what this choice cost.
