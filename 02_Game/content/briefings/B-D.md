This chapter adds the government, which buys about a fifth of everything the economy produces, for example roads, schools and hospitals. When the government spends more for a while, demand rises, and the economy needs a higher policy rate to keep output at its potential; the shorter people expect the extra spending to last, the stronger this effect. Later in the chapter, two parties will compete for office in elections.
