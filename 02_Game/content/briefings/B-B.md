In the last chapter, every shock came from spending, and a change in spending moves inflation and output in the same direction, both up in a boom and both down in a slowdown, so one policy rate could bring both back to their targets at the same time. In this chapter, firms' costs can change on their own, for example when energy becomes more expensive. Higher costs push inflation up and output down, so the two move in opposite directions, and no single policy rate can bring both back at once. Your mandate says how much weight each one gets, and the debrief at the end of the term shows what each choice cost.
