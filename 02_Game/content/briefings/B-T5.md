The public believes we are aiming a little above our {law.target} percent inflation target, so it expects higher inflation, and that keeps inflation up. Bringing this belief down costs output for a while. Announce a plan of policy rates for the coming quarters that the public can rely on: a plan the public believes brings its expectations down at a smaller cost. If new information arrives later, you can change the plan without losing the public's trust, as far as the new information justifies. If nothing new has happened, the staff forecast may still show that leaving the plan would improve this quarter's figures, but what it does not show is the cost: the public would trust our next announcements less. Ask me whenever you want to weigh a decision.
