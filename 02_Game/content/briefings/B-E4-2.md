A long slump has begun: households and firms expect weak demand for many quarters, and the rate the economy needs has fallen sharply. Our usual policy would now set the policy rate below zero, which is not possible, so the rate stays at zero, and the public expects it to stay there for {public.spell} quarters. If we promise to keep it at zero for longer than that, the public's expectations change and the slump becomes milder. A promise that only repeats what the public already expects changes nothing.
