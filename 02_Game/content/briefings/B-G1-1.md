Households and firms have started to spend more than usual: a demand shock, a change in spending that does not come from our policy, and if we did nothing, output would rise above its potential and prices would start to rise faster. We therefore set the policy rate to {guided.rate} percent, {guided.change}. A higher rate makes borrowing more expensive and slows spending. You will see the effect in next quarter's figures.
