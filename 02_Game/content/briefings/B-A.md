Welcome to the central bank. As its governor, you decide one number each quarter: the policy rate, the short-term interest rate the central bank sets, and everything else on your desk helps you choose it. The real interest rate is the policy rate minus the inflation people expect. When it is lower than the rate the economy needs, the real interest rate at which spending neither grows nor shrinks, households and firms spend more, output rises above its potential, prices and wages rise faster and unemployment falls, and when it is higher the opposite happens. These effects take time: a decision you take this quarter shows in next quarter's figures, and wages keep adjusting for several quarters after that. I will brief you at the start of each term and whenever something important happens.
